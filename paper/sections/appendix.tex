\section{The Cut Algorithm}\label{sec:cut-algorithm}
Algorithm \ref{alg:wl-cut} outlines the feature extraction procedure for \texttt{WLc-n}. The edge-enhanced variant \texttt{WLc-ne} operates identically, except that edge types are included alongside neighbour colours during aggregation (Line \ref{algline:agg}).

\begin{algorithm}[t]
\small
\caption{\texttt{WLc-n} Algorithm for Feature Extraction.}
\label{alg:wl-cut}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require Directed graph $G = (V, E)$, iterations $T \geq 1$, cut nodes $V_{cut} \subseteq V$.
\Ensure Feature histograms of colours and pairs.
\State \textbf{Initialisation:} $c^{(0)}(v) \leftarrow \text{HASH}(\text{type}(v) \,||\, \text{subtype}(v))$ for all $v \in V$ \label{algline:init}
\State $t \leftarrow 0$
\Repeat
    \State $t \leftarrow t + 1$
    \For{each node $v \in V$}
        \If{$v \notin V_{cut}$} \label{algline:ifcut}
            \State $M(v) \leftarrow \{\!\!\{c^{(t-1)}(w) \mid (w,v) \in E\}\!\!\}$ \label{algline:agg}
            \State $c^{(t)}(v) \leftarrow \text{HASH}(c^{(t-1)}(v), \text{sort}(M(v)))$ \label{algline:hash}
        \Else
            \State $c^{(t)}(v) \leftarrow c^{(t-1)}(v)$
        \EndIf
    \EndFor
\Until{$t = T$}
\State $Pairs \leftarrow \{\!\!\{\}\!\!\}$ \Comment{Multiset for pair occurrences} \label{algline:pairsinit}
\For{each node $v \in V_{cut}$} \label{algline:forcut}
    \State $S(v) \leftarrow \{ c^{(0)}(w) \mid (w,v) \in E \}$ \Comment{Set of initial incoming neighbour types} \label{algline:set}
    \State $c^{(final)}(v) \leftarrow \text{HASH}(c^{(T)}(v), \text{sort}(S(v)))$ \label{algline:cfinalcut}
    \For{each node $w$ where $(w,v) \in E$} \label{algline:forinw}
        \State $Pairs \leftarrow Pairs \cup \{\!\!\{ (c^{(0)}(w), c^{(0)}(v)) \}\!\!\}$ \label{algline:pairsadd}
    \EndFor
\EndFor
\State $c^{(final)}(v) \leftarrow c^{(T)}(v)$ for all $v \in V \setminus V_{cut}$ \label{algline:cfinalnoncut}
\State \Return $\text{Histogram}(c^{(final)}) \mathbin{||} \text{Histogram}(Pairs)$ \label{algline:return}
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

The execution of Algorithm \ref{alg:wl-cut} operates as follows:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Line \ref{algline:init} (Initialisation):} Each node $v \in V$ receives an initial colour $c^{(0)}(v)$ based on its type and subtype (e.g., variable, parameter, or operator/constraint type).
    \item \textbf{Lines 3--14 (Iterative Refinement):} Standard Weisfeiler-Leman refinement runs for $T$ steps over non-cut nodes ($v \notin V_{cut}$), updating their colour by hashing the sorted multiset $M(v)$ of incoming neighbour colours (Lines \ref{algline:agg}--\ref{algline:hash}). Cut nodes skip this aggregation (Line \ref{algline:ifcut}), preventing degree-dependent updates, but continue to propagate their colours downstream.
    \item \textbf{Lines \ref{algline:forcut}--\ref{algline:pairsadd} (Cut Node Aggregation \& Pair Counting):} After refinement, cut nodes aggregate the \emph{set} (rather than multiset) $S(v)$ of their incoming neighbour types (Line \ref{algline:set}), updating their final colour in Line \ref{algline:cfinalcut}. This ensures cut nodes of the same type with the same set of incoming node types receive the same colour regardless of arity. Lines \ref{algline:forinw}--\ref{algline:pairsadd} recover the discarded degree information by collecting directed $(neighbour, cut)$ pairs into $Pairs$.
    \item \textbf{Lines \ref{algline:cfinalnoncut}--\ref{algline:return} (Feature Extraction):} Non-cut nodes retain their refined colours $c^{(T)}(v)$, and the final feature vector is formed by concatenating the histograms of node colours and neighbour pairs.
\end{itemize}

\section{On The Shallowness of the Graphs}\label{sec:graph-depth}
Figure \ref{fig:long-graph} shows the constraint that produces the deepest path currently implemented. A family of similar constraints is present, and they usually differ in the type of constraint that replaces the equality (e.g. inequality, less than or equal to, etc.) This local decomposition has a longest directed path of 4 edges, with effective propagation depth reduced to 2 when the cut is applied. Most supported constraints are, instead, quite simple, with most of them having directed paths of at most one or two edges. For the full list of supported constraints, we refer to the FlatZinc specification.\footnote{\url{https://docs.minizinc.dev/en/stable/lib-flatzinc.html}}

\section{Ablation Study on the Importance of Concatenating All Aggregation Levels}\label{sec:ablation-study}
To assess whether only using the last aggregation level of the \texttt{WL} resulted in a loss of performance, we conducted an ablation study on the best-performing features: \texttt{WLc-n-1/2}, \texttt{WLc-ne-1/2}, \texttt{WLn-1/2}, and \texttt{WLne-1/2} across all different tasks and ML models. Results can be found in Tables \ref{tab:all-levels-borda} (Borda score), \ref{tab:all-levels-accuracy} (algorithm selection accuracy), and \ref{tab:all-levels-par} (parallel prediction). The tables show the mean and median results of the ML models trained on only the last level or on all levels of aggregation, as well as the p-value computed with the Wilcoxon test. In all cases but one, the results are not statistically significant. The only case in which they are (\texttt{WLc-n-1} in SVM algorithm selection accuracy prediction), it is in favour of the features with only the last aggregation level.
